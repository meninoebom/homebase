# Reading List

Split by purpose. Use both, for different reasons.

## For grants, positioning, and crumb.blog essay voice

1. **Curtis Roads, *Composing Electronic Music: A New Aesthetic*** (2015). Roads on time scales from microsound to form. Vocabulary for what Ralf's translators should care about at each scale.
2. **Rudolf Laban, *The Mastery of Movement*** (Ullmann rev.). Effort-Shape IS the quality extraction layer. Read the source so the vocabulary stops being borrowed.
3. **Mark Johnson, *The Meaning of the Body*.** Makes "dance is the intelligent system" defensible philosophically rather than poetic.
4. **Lucy Suchman, *Human-Machine Reconfigurations*** (2nd ed of *Plans and Situated Actions*). Situated action is the stick to beat "toolkit" framings with. Wekinator is plans. Ralf is situated.
5. **Iannis Xenakis, *Formalized Music*,** stochastic and sieves chapters. Not the math. The stance: a composer inventing his own formalism because existing ones could not hold his intuition.
6. **David Rokeby, "Transforming Mirrors"** (1995 essay, free online). The clearest articulation of interactive art as a relational form. Ralf's direct ancestor.
7. **Paul Dourish, *Where the Action Is*.** Embodied interaction for HCI. Bridges Suchman to the creative coding world.

Optional eighth: **Susan Foster, *Choreographing Empathy*** for the dance side deeper.

## For frontier technical currency (for Track 3 reactivation)

- **Jesse Engel et al, DDSP (ICLR 2020).** Required. Read the paper before any Track 3 rep.
- **EnCodec paper** plus running MusicGen once locally. Feel the latency wall.
- **MotionGPT paper.** For the discrete-latent direction in motion representation.
- **Temporal or Inngest durable execution primer.** For the agent handoff problem (Track 2).
- **MCP specification docs.** For Track 2.

## For historical permission (creative coding lineage)

Five figures whose careers should give Brandon confidence that the positioning is the asset, not the handicap:

- **Laurie Spiegel** (Bell Labs, Music Mouse): refused to separate engineering from composing. Both were the work.
- **David Rokeby** (Very Nervous System, 1986 onward): no CS degree narrative. The work is the credential.
- **Rebecca Fiebrink** (Wekinator): started as a musician, finished a CS PhD framed entirely around the musicians' workflow. Made the "weird" framing legible to both worlds without flattening either.
- **Michel Waisvisz** (The Hands, 1984, STEIM director): built instruments nobody asked for and ran an institution on that conviction for decades.
- **Miller Puckette** (Max and Pure Data): career built inside IRCAM and UCSD on the bet that the right abstraction for a composer is a patchable dataflow graph. That bet paid off for a generation of artists.

Each of them would look "weird and non-hirable" to a 2026 full-stack recruiter. Each of them built a career on not flattening themselves.
