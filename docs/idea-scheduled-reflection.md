## Idea Brief: The Scheduled Reflection Moment (feedback inside the cadence)

**Problem:** The `/integrations` "Use with AI" flow is genuinely well built. It assembles a clean digest of your last 30 days plus strategy files, hands you a primer that teaches any agent your file layout, and keeps everything on-device until you choose to paste. But it lives behind a footer link on `/` labeled "Use with AI," next to "Customize," and nobody remembers a footer link exists. The reflection is available and it is never triggered, because nothing in the daily or weekly rhythm ever points at it. It is a room with the lights off at the end of a hallway you never walk down.

Ira Progoff's insight, the one Homebase's positioning revives, was that feedback belongs *inside* the practice cadence, not off to the side as an optional tool. You read the journal back to yourself as part of the ritual, on a rhythm, not when you happen to remember there is a feature for it. A reflection you have to remember to seek out is a reflection you will not have.

**Solution:** Fuse the AI reflection to the **weekly review threshold** from `docs/idea-rollover-review-ritual.md`. When a user crosses into a new week and lands on the review threshold ("what carries, what is done, what do you drop?"), offer one more affordance right there: a single button, **"Reflect on last week."** Pressing it copies the same digest the `/integrations` page produces, plus the three sharp questions from the reflection skill, to the clipboard, so the user pastes it into whatever AI they already use. The reflection stops being a footer errand and becomes a beat in the weekly ritual, arriving at the exact moment a person is already looking back.

Critically, this is **choreography of things that already exist**, not new machinery. The digest assembler (`gatherDigest`), the primer, and the reflection skill are all built and shipping. The idea is to place a copy-to-clipboard button at the review threshold that assembles those existing pieces into one paste. No inference, no model, no API, no persona.

**For whom:** Brandon first, then anyone who keeps a Homebase and wants the "journal that talks back" experience without having to remember that a tool for it exists. People who do a weekly review anyway and would take the AI reflection *if it were handed to them at the review*, but will never navigate to a footer for it.

**Why now:** Two things just became true. First, the reflection primitives shipped: `gatherDigest` in `homebase/src/lib/reflect/digest.ts`, the `/integrations` copy flow, and the portable skill in `skill/homebase-reflection/SKILL.md`. Second, Brief 1 proposes a rhythmic threshold at each new period, which is the missing *place* for the reflection to live. The reflection has been homeless; the rollover threshold gives it a home. Building this now is almost free because it reuses the digest path wholesale and only adds one button plus one string.

## Relationship to the deferred-inference decision (this is the lived-experience test)

`docs/idea-reflection-slots.md` made a deliberate decision in June 2026: **build the shape, defer the inference.** Brandon will keep using the manual copy-digest-into-Claude path and let lived experience teach him which reflections are worth having before wiring any model. This brief is fully consistent with that decision, and it is arguably *the concrete form of it.*

- **It adds zero inference.** Nothing here calls a model. It copies text to the clipboard, exactly as `/integrations` does today. The user still chooses where to paste it, and nothing leaves the folder until they do. So this does not jump the gun on the deferred decision; it stays firmly on the manual side of the line the reflection-slots brief drew.
- **It is the manual path made rhythmic.** The reflection-slots decision says: keep using the manual flow and *let lived experience teach you which reflections are worth having every morning.* But a manual flow buried in a footer generates almost no lived experience, because it is almost never used. Bringing the manual reflection to the weekly threshold is precisely what generates that lived experience. It is the experiment the deferred-inference decision asked for, given a cadence so it actually runs.
- **When inference eventually arrives, this is where it plugs in.** The reflection-slots brief specifies a "reflection provider" socket, with a local-WebGPU model as the intended default and a clearly-labeled opt-in hosted provider as a power-user option. This brief's button is the *manual provider*: "assemble the paste, hand it to the human, the human is the transport." A future local-model provider swaps in behind the same button ("Reflect on last week" runs the model in-browser and shows the result) without moving the affordance. So the choreography built here is forward-compatible with the socket that brief describes; we are wiring the human-in-the-loop version of the exact same seam.

In one line: reflection-slots decided *what* the eventual feature is and chose to defer the model; this brief decides *when and where* the manual version fires, which is the only way to earn the lived experience that unlocks the model later.

## Mechanics

1. **What the button assembles.** The paste is two parts, both already built:
   - **The digest.** Call `gatherDigest()` (`homebase/src/lib/reflect/digest.ts`). Today it defaults to 30 days; for a weekly reflection the natural window is the last week (roughly 7 days), so pass `{ days: 7 }` or a "last week" window anchored on the period boundary. The assembler is already pure and parameterized on `days`, so this needs no new code, just a different argument. Strategy files (values, the horizon files) come along automatically, which is what lets the reflection check lived days against stated intent.
   - **The three sharp questions from the skill.** `skill/homebase-reflection/SKILL.md` already contains the exact stance and its "good starting prompt": *don't summarize; tell me one pattern I'm not seeing, one place my days are drifting from my values; be direct.* Prepend that framing to the digest so the paste is self-contained: a person can paste it into a blank chat and get a real reflection, not a summary. This is the same primer discipline `/integrations` uses, tuned to "last week" and to the three questions rather than the open-ended primer.
2. **Where the button lives.** At the review threshold from Brief 1, rendered inside the expanded week `HorizonRow` alongside the "what carries / what is done" review invitation. It is a peer of the review prompt, not a separate screen: you are already looking back at last week; "reflect on last week with an AI" is the natural third option next to "keep this / drop this."
3. **What pressing it does (MVP).** Exactly what `/integrations` "Copy last 30 days" does: assemble in the browser, write to the clipboard, show a "Copied" confirmation and a one-line instruction ("Paste this into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant"). No navigation away, no model call, no network. The `copyDigest` handler in `homebase/src/routes/integrations.tsx` is the working reference implementation, including its busy state and its "couldn't read your folder" error handling, both of which should be reused rather than reinvented.
4. **Cadence, honestly.** This fires *at the weekly threshold*, which is once per new week per Brief 1's once-per-boundary guarantee. It is not a daily nag and not an always-present button competing for attention on the cover. It shows up when you are already reviewing, then gets out of the way. If Brief 1's threshold is dismissed without reflecting, that is fine; the `/integrations` page stays as the always-available manual path for anyone who wants to reflect off-cadence.
5. **On-ethos, restated.** Nothing leaves the folder until the user pastes it somewhere themselves. The digest is assembled entirely in the browser and only ever reaches the clipboard (the exact posture documented at the top of `digest.ts`). This brief adds a *when* and a *where*, and changes none of that posture.

## The dependency, stated plainly

**This brief depends on Brief 1.** The whole value is that the reflection arrives *at the weekly review threshold*, and that threshold is what `docs/idea-rollover-review-ritual.md` proposes. Without Brief 1, this degrades to "add a second copy button somewhere," which is just more footer, the exact problem we are solving. So the sequencing is: Brief 1 builds the rollover detection and the threshold screen; this brief adds one button to that screen. If Brief 1 is not built, the honest fallback is to leave the reflection where it is on `/integrations` and not pretend a second entry point fixes discoverability.

There is a soft dependency the other way, too: Brief 1's "does the threshold earn its interruption?" question and this brief's "does the rhythmic reflection get used?" question are the *same lived-experience experiment*, run on the same weekly beat. Building them together is how you learn whether the whole review-plus-reflect ritual is worth the interruption, which is exactly the evidence the deferred-inference decision in `docs/idea-reflection-slots.md` is waiting on.

## File pointers (the real seams)

- **The digest, already built and parameterized:** `homebase/src/lib/reflect/digest.ts`. `gatherDigest({ days })` and the pure `buildDigest(files, opts)` do all the assembly. Pass a 7-day (last-week) window for the weekly reflection. No new assembly code.
- **The stance and the three questions:** `skill/homebase-reflection/SKILL.md`. Its "good starting prompt" (don't summarize; one unseen pattern; one drift from values; be direct) is the exact framing to prepend. Keep it in sync with `homebase/src/lib/reflect/primer.ts`, the same way the reflection-slots brief notes the primer and skill are kept in sync.
- **The working copy-to-clipboard reference:** `homebase/src/routes/integrations.tsx`, the `copyDigest` handler. Reuse its browser-assembly, busy state, "Copied ✓" confirmation, and folder-read error handling. This button is a second, cadence-placed caller of the same path.
- **Where the button mounts:** `homebase/src/components/HorizonRow.tsx`, inside the expanded week row at the review threshold (Brief 1's third render branch). It sits next to the review invitation, not on the footer.
- **Where the threshold is decided:** `homebase/src/routes/index.tsx` (`StrategyAccordion`) plus the `homebase.lastSeenPeriod.*` detection from Brief 1. The reflect button is only offered when the threshold is staged.
- **The forward-compatible socket:** the "reflection provider" abstraction described in `docs/idea-reflection-slots.md`. The MVP provider here is "the human carries the paste"; a future local-WebGPU provider swaps in behind the same button without moving it.

## Post-MVP knobs (roadmap, not now)

1. **Show the reflection in-app instead of copying out.** Once the local-WebGPU provider from `docs/idea-reflection-slots.md` exists, "Reflect on last week" runs the model in-browser and renders the observation inline at the threshold, with the copy-out button demoted to a fallback for people who prefer their own frontier model. Same affordance, model behind it. This is the moment the deferred inference gets un-deferred, and only after the manual version has proven the reflection is wanted.
2. **Persist the reflection into the week file.** The reflection-slots brief argues a reflection is worth keeping (it is the journal talking about you). If the reflection eventually renders in-app, write it as a machine-authored section into the week's strategy file, so past reflections compound and become context for future ones. Skip until there is an in-app reflection to persist.
3. **Provider socket, explicitly opt-in hosted option.** A clearly-labeled "this sends your last week to X for a sharper reflection" hosted provider, never the default. Same decision, and same caution, as the reflection-slots brief; a separate call, made only after the on-device path exists.
4. **Per-horizon reflection windows.** A monthly threshold could offer "reflect on last month" (30-day window, the current `gatherDigest` default) and a yearly one a longer arc. The digest is already parameterized on `days`, so this is an argument per horizon, not new code. Add once the weekly version has earned it.
